# NRI & Wealth Management Relationship Playbook

## 01. Cross-Border Banking & NRI Accounts
- **Summary**: Comprehensive comparison of NRE, NRO, and FCNR accounts, repatriation limits, FEMA regulations, and double taxation avoidance agreements (DTAA).
- **Context**:
  - lessons/01-cross-border-banking/01-nre-nro-framework.md
  - lessons/01-cross-border-banking/02-fema-tax-compliance.md
- **Knowledge Check**:
  - **Question**: Which type of bank account allows unrestricted, 100% principal and interest repatriation to overseas jurisdictions without requiring a CA Form 15CA/15CB certificate?
  - **Type**: multiple_choice
  - **Options**:
    - Non-Resident Ordinary (NRO) Savings Account
    - Resident Foreign Currency Domestic (RFCD) Account
    - [x] Non-Resident External (NRE) Rupee Account
    - Special Non-Resident Rupee (SNRR) Account
  - **Explanation**: NRE account balances (both principal remittance inflows and earned interest) are freely repatriable outside India without any annual dollar limit or statutory tax withholding certificates.
- **Knowledge Check**:
  - **Question**: What is the primary difference in taxation between interest earned on an NRE Fixed Deposit versus an NRO Fixed Deposit?
  - **Type**: multiple_choice
  - **Options**:
    - Both NRE and NRO interest are taxed at 30% TDS flat
    - [x] NRE interest is completely tax-free in India, whereas NRO interest is subject to TDS
    - NRO interest is tax-free while NRE interest is taxed at slab rates
    - Neither account earns any interest under RBI directives
  - **Explanation**: Interest earned on NRE accounts is exempt from income tax in India under Section 10(4)(ii) of the Income Tax Act. In contrast, interest earned on NRO deposits is taxable in India and subject to TDS.
- **Knowledge Check**:
  - **Question**: Can a Non-Resident Indian (NRI) maintain foreign currency without exchange risk in an Indian bank?
  - **Type**: multiple_choice
  - **Options**:
    - No, all NRI funds must be converted to Indian Rupees immediately upon arrival
    - [x] Yes, through a Foreign Currency Non-Resident (FCNR-B) deposit maintained in designated currencies
    - Only by opening an offshore account outside India
    - Only if the individual has held NRI status for more than 10 years
  - **Explanation**: FCNR(B) deposits allow NRIs to hold fixed deposits in freely convertible foreign currencies (USD, GBP, EUR, etc.), fully eliminating currency exchange fluctuation risk for the depositor.

## 02. LRS Remittances & Overseas Education Financing
- **Summary**: Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) $250k annual window, Form A2 declaration workflows, Tax Collected at Source (TCS) rules, and structuring high-ticket overseas student financing.
- **Context**:
  - lessons/02-lrs-remittances/01-lrs-mechanics.md
  - lessons/02-lrs-remittances/02-form-a2-and-tcs.md
- **Knowledge Check**:
  - **Question**: Under current RBI Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) regulations, what is the statutory Tax Collected at Source (TCS) rate for education remittances exceeding INR 7 Lakhs when funded via a recognized financial institution education loan?
  - **Type**: multiple_choice
  - **Options**:
    - 20% flat rate on total remittance
    - 5% on amount exceeding INR 7 Lakhs
    - [x] 0.5% on amount exceeding INR 7 Lakhs
    - Exempt up to USD 250,000 with zero TCS
  - **Explanation**: When education remittances above INR 7 Lakh are sourced from a loan obtained from an authorized financial institution under Section 80E, a concessional TCS rate of 0.5% applies (compared to 5% for self-funded education and 20% for other unclassified remittances).
- **Knowledge Check**:
  - **Question**: What is the maximum annual remittance limit allowed per financial year under the RBI Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) for a resident individual?
  - **Type**: multiple_choice
  - **Options**:
    - USD 100,000
    - [x] USD 250,000 (or its equivalent)
    - USD 500,000
    - Unlimited for all capital account transactions
  - **Explanation**: Under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS), resident individuals are freely permitted to remit up to USD 250,000 per financial year (April-March) for permissible current and capital account transactions.
- **Knowledge Check**:
  - **Question**: Which statutory document must a resident individual execute with an Authorized Dealer bank before executing an overseas remittance under LRS?
  - **Type**: multiple_choice
  - **Options**:
    - Form 16A only
    - [x] Form A2 cum LRS Declaration
    - FATCA Self-Certification Form 60
    - RBI Form FC-GPR
  - **Explanation**: Remitters under LRS must furnish Form A2 along with a declaration confirming that the total remittances drawn through all authorized dealers during the financial year do not exceed the USD 250,000 limit.

## 03. Mortgage Advisory & Rate Reset Retention
- **Summary**: Strategies for preserving prime home loan books during repo-rate tightening cycles, proactive client communication, benchmark spread adjustments, and countering balance transfer raids.
- **Context**:
  - lessons/03-mortgage-retention/01-rate-reset-dynamics.md
  - lessons/03-mortgage-retention/02-client-negotiation-playbook.md
- **Knowledge Check**:
  - **Question**: When a prime HNI mortgage customer receives a competitive balance transfer offer at 40 bps lower than their current floating rate, what is the most value-accretive initial retention response for the RM?
  - **Type**: multiple_choice
  - **Options**:
    - Immediately issue a foreclosure certificate to accelerate branch recovery
    - Require the customer to submit a physical complaint at the home branch
    - [x] Analyze total switching costs (MODT, stamp duty, processing fees) and offer an internal spread-reset conversion with fee waiver
    - Offer an uncollateralized personal loan at promotional rates
  - **Explanation**: Switching lenders incurs substantial transaction friction (title verification, mortgage registration/MODT stamp duty of 0.2-0.5%, and processing fees). Calculating the true breakeven payback period and presenting an instant internal rate-reset conversion frequently saves the relationship at zero net cost to the customer.
- **Knowledge Check**:
  - **Question**: What is a major component of lender switching costs that an RM should calculate when counseling a customer considering a Home Loan Balance Transfer?
  - **Type**: multiple_choice
  - **Options**:
    - Advance tax payments
    - [x] Memorandum of Deposit of Title Deeds (MODT) stamp duty, legal evaluation fees, and new processing fees
    - GST on original property registration
    - Penalty fees for early closure of savings accounts
  - **Explanation**: A balance transfer requires creating a new mortgage with the incoming lender, which incurs MODT stamp duty charges (varying by state, usually 0.2% to 0.5% of loan amount), technical inspection fees, and loan processing fees that often offset marginal interest rate reductions.
- **Knowledge Check**:
  - **Question**: Under RBI guidelines, are banks permitted to levy foreclosure charges or pre-payment penalties on floating rate home loans sanctioned to individual borrowers?
  - **Type**: multiple_choice
  - **Options**:
    - Yes, up to 2% of the outstanding principal balance
    - [x] No, foreclosure charges and prepayment penalties are strictly prohibited on all floating rate term loans to individual borrowers
    - Only if the loan has been active for less than 3 years
    - Yes, but only for loans exceeding INR 1 Crore
  - **Explanation**: The Reserve Bank of India has prohibited banks from charging any foreclosure charges or prepayment penalties on floating rate term loans sanctioned to individual borrowers (with or without co-obligants).

## 04. Saathi Institutional Memory & RM Transitions
- **Summary**: Best practices for zero-loss relationship transitions, documenting implicit client preferences, family wealth milestones, and utilizing Saathi's contextual memory for advisory continuity.
- **Context**:
  - lessons/04-saathi-transitions/01-rm-handoff-protocol.md
  - lessons/04-saathi-transitions/02-relationship-intelligence.md
- **Knowledge Check**:
  - **Question**: How does the Saathi Relationship Intelligence platform prevent client attrition during RM portfolio reassignments?
  - **Type**: multiple_choice
  - **Options**:
    - By automatically closing inactive deposit accounts after 90 days
    - By transferring client communication exclusively to an unassisted IVR menu
    - [x] By synthesizing unstructured interaction notes, family financial milestones, and implicit service sensitivities into an instantly actionable profile for the successor RM
    - By locking customer accounts until an in-person KYC re-verification is completed
  - **Explanation**: RM turnover traditionally causes high attrition because personal context is lost. Saathi captures nuances (such as preferred meeting times, upcoming offspring college enrollments, or specific property liquidity timelines) to give incoming bankers day-one relational rapport.
- **Knowledge Check**:
  - **Question**: Why is tracking conversational sentiment and client personal milestones critical in the Saathi portal?
  - **Type**: multiple_choice
  - **Options**:
    - It is required by regulatory credit rating agencies
    - [x] It allows relationship managers to provide proactive, timely advisory touchpoints and preserve relationship equity across team transitions
    - It determines the legal jurisdiction of the client's account
    - It replaces mandatory annual AML screening
  - **Explanation**: Tracking personal milestones and advisory notes within Saathi ensures that relationship equity is institutionalized rather than lost when relationship managers change, resulting in sustained client trust and timely cross-sell opportunities.
- **Knowledge Check**:
  - **Question**: When an RM portfolio transfer occurs, what is the best practice recommended in Saathi's transition playbook?
  - **Type**: multiple_choice
  - **Options**:
    - Send an automated generic SMS to the customer without introductory calls
    - [x] Schedule a joint introductory call or meeting between the outgoing RM, incoming RM, and client, referencing prior Saathi notes and preferences
    - Re-run full credit underwriting before contacting the client
    - Request the client to visit the branch to re-introduce themselves
  - **Explanation**: The playbook recommends a warm handoff, ideally an introductory joint call or meeting where the new RM demonstrates prior knowledge of the client's history and investment priorities sourced from Saathi memory.
