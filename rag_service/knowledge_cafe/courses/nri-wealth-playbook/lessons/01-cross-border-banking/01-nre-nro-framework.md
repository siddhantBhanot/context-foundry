# Non-Resident External (NRE) vs Non-Resident Ordinary (NRO) Banking Framework

## Executive Summary
For Indian banks catering to Global Indians (NRIs, OCIs, and Persons of Indian Origin), properly classifying deposit accounts under the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), 1999 is critical for regulatory compliance and tax optimization.

## Comparative Matrix

| Attribute | NRE Account (Rupee) | NRO Account (Rupee) | FCNR(B) Account |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Denomination Currency** | Indian Rupee (INR) | Indian Rupee (INR) | Foreign Currency (USD, GBP, EUR, etc.) |
| **Permissible Credits** | Inward remittances from abroad, transfer from other NRE/FCNR accounts | Legitimate Indian earnings (rent, dividends, pension, sale of domestic assets), inward remittances | Direct inward remittances in foreign currency |
| **Repatriability** | 100% principal & interest freely repatriable | Up to USD 1 Million per financial year subject to Form 15CA/15CB tax clearance | 100% principal & interest freely repatriable with zero currency risk |
| **Taxation in India** | Completely exempt from Indian Income Tax | Subject to TDS at 30% plus applicable surcharge & cess (relief available under DTAA) | Completely exempt from Indian Income Tax |
| **Joint Account Holding** | Allowed with another NRI/OCI or with a resident relative on "Former or Survivor" basis | Allowed with any resident relative or non-resident on joint/either/survivor basis | Allowed with another NRI |

## Advisory Workflow for Relationship Managers
1. **Source of Funds Assessment**: When an NRI deposits domestic Indian earnings (e.g. rental income from Gurgaon properties), guide them to the NRO account. Inward remittances from overseas payroll accounts should be directed to NRE or FCNR(B).
2. **Repatriation Guidance**: For clients repatriating estate proceeds or property sales from NRO accounts, pre-clear the CA certificate (Form 15CB) and income tax declaration (Form 15CA) to prevent remittance turnaround delays.
